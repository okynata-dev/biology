What the cell does

No promise of returns, no pooled fund. The asset is in the cell from the first block.

01 · Mint

Inoculation

Part of the mint buys biotech stock tokens, and they go straight into a wallet the NFT itself owns. You are not owed the share later. It is already there.

02 · Hold

The cytoplasm

Each token has its own on-chain wallet, derived from the collection and the id. Sell the cell and the contents leave with it. Nobody custodies it for you.

03 · Culture

Metabolism

Lock $BIO against a cell and it starts working. A dormant cell keeps its art and its inoculum but secretes nothing.

04 · Earn

Secretion

Creator earnings from every secondary sale buy more biotech tokens, which drop into cultured cells by growth phase.

The original plan routed fees through an NFT AMM. Without one we fund it the plain way: the mint pays for the inoculum, secondary earnings pay for the secretion. Slower than a 15% swap fee, and it depends on nobody but us.

The inoculum

Which share a cell is born holding follows its shape, not a coin flip.

Spheres draw from the vaccine makers, helices from the gene editors, rods from big pharma. Scarcity then means something twice over: the sprite is rare, and so is what it carries.

The whole colony

Every one of the 4,444, drawn in your browser from the same rules that generate the collection.

Rarity is the summed inverse frequency of each trait, computed over the finished set. Nothing is assigned by hand, so the scarce ones are scarce because the weights made them scarce.
